    What do SAIDI, SAIFI and CAIDI mean?

    SAIDI is the total time the average customer is without power in a year. We show it in hours.

    SAIFI is how many times the average customer loses power in a year.

    CAIDI is how long a single outage lasts on average. It equals SAIDI divided by SAIFI.

    LUMA calculates these using the IEEE 1366 industry standard, which leaves out major storms, planned outages and outages caused by power plants.

    How we grade

    Each measure earns up to 100 points, based on where LUMA’s result falls between a starting line and a goal.

    The starting line is the Energy Bureau’s official baseline: how the old public utility, PREPA, performed from July 2019 to June 2020, before LUMA took over. Where no official baseline exists, we use LUMA’s first full year, July 2021 to June 2022.

    The goal is the Energy Bureau’s benchmark, the level it wants the grid to reach. Where there is none, the goal is 25% better than the starting line.

    A result at the starting line earns 60 points, a D: no better than before. Reaching the goal earns 100. A result 50% worse than the starting line, or more, earns 0.

    The overall grade is a weighted average. Measures the operating contract treats as most important, like outage time, outage frequency and worker deaths, count the most.

    Important: this is an independent grade, not an official rating from the Energy Bureau. The weights are our own, grouped by the areas in LUMA’s operating contract; the Bureau’s official incentive weights are not used.

    About the data

    Where it comes from

    Every number on this page comes from LUMA’s monthly performance report, filed with the Puerto Rico Energy Bureau in case NEPR-MI-2019-0007. This version uses the report LUMA filed in August 2026, with data from June 2021 through July 2026. The most recent month is preliminary and may be revised.

    Why the report exists

    LUMA took over the transmission and distribution system from PREPA in June 2021 under an operation and maintenance contract. The Energy Bureau uses this report to check whether service is improving. It can order LUMA to explain measures that got worse and can impose fines when the explanation falls short.

    Baselines and goals

    The baselines and benchmarks were set by the Energy Bureau in May 2021. Separately, LUMA’s contract pays an incentive fee for meeting targets the Bureau sets in another case, NEPR-AP-2020-0025. This report card does not use those incentive targets.
